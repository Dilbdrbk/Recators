Frequently Asked Questions About Buying Used Reactors

Answers to the most common questions from procurement and project teams evaluating used batch reactors from IPP.

What is a chemical reactor?

+
A chemical reactor is a pressure vessel in which a controlled chemical reaction takes place. It holds the reactants, contains the pressure and temperature the reaction needs, and transfers heat in or out through a jacket or coil. In process plants a reactor is defined by four things: its capacity, its material of construction, its internal pressure rating and its internal temperature rating. Everything else, including agitation, sealing and instrumentation, is selected around those four.

What are the main types of chemical reactors?

+
Process reactors fall into two families. Batch reactors hold a fixed charge for the duration of the reaction and are emptied between runs, which suits pharmaceutical, fine chemical and specialty production where recipes change. Continuous reactors keep reactants flowing through, which suits high volume single product duty. Within those families IPP stocks batch-type agitated, batch type body only, fixed bed, fluid bed, hydrogenation, polymerization and tubular designs.

How does a batch reactor work?

+
The vessel is charged with reactants, sealed, then brought to the required temperature by circulating a heating or cooling medium through the jacket. An agitator driven from the top keeps the contents mixed and the heat transfer even. The reaction runs to completion under controlled pressure and temperature, the batch is discharged, and the vessel is cleaned for the next run. Mixing quality is set by the impeller type, and containment is set by the shaft seal.

What is the difference between a batch reactor and a continuous reactor?

+
A batch reactor processes one fixed charge at a time and is emptied between runs. A continuous reactor, such as a tubular or fixed bed design, has reactants flowing through it without interruption. Batch equipment is more flexible across products and is normal in pharmaceutical and specialty chemical plants. Continuous equipment gives more consistent output at higher volume for a single product.

What is a glass-lined reactor and when is it used?

+
A glass-lined reactor is a steel vessel with a fused glass lining on every wetted surface. The steel carries the pressure and the glass carries the corrosion resistance, which lets the vessel handle acids and aggressive intermediates that would attack stainless steel. It is the standard choice in pharmaceutical, agrochemical and fine chemical duty. Worn linings can be stripped and re-glassed to factory specification rather than the vessel being scrapped.

What does jacketed mean on a reactor?

+
A jacket is an outer chamber around the vessel wall through which steam, hot oil, chilled water or glycol is circulated to heat or cool the contents. The jacket carries its own separate pressure and temperature rating, which is why listings quote internal and jacket ratings independently. A reactor cannot be selected on internal rating alone if the duty depends on the heat transfer the jacket provides.

What does reactor capacity mean on a listing?

+
Listed capacity is the design or nominal volume of the vessel, not the working volume. Usable batch size is normally lower, because the charge has to sit below the agitator sweep and leave headspace for mixing, foaming and expansion. When matching a reactor to a process, compare the required working volume against the listed design capacity with that margin in mind.

+

Body only reactors are reactor shells without agitation components. Both subtypes are available in multiple materials of construction and condition grades.
